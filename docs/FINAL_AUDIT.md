## 10. Live play (Step N)

- **What it is:** menu → Play with friends. One player hosts a 6-digit room, friends join with the code. The host picks days, seconds per day, capital and scenario and presses Start; every device starts the same seeded game together after a 5 s countdown. Same seed means the same market for everyone (tested: two devices produce byte-identical saves).
- **Transport:** Supabase Realtime Broadcast + Presence (no tables, nothing stored). Logic is in `js/live.js`, separate from the transport, so it is tested with an in-memory bus (`tests/live.test.js`, 15 tests: lobby, start sync, host-only start and events, forged messages ignored, late joiners turned away, ranking, spoofed score ids, bad values, leaving, transport failure).
- **Browser check:** two real browser pages played a full room against a stub of the Supabase client (BroadcastChannel): lobby, start, same settings, event from host reaches guest, leaderboard, no pause, no page errors.
- **Not verified:** the real Supabase network connection. The sandbox cannot reach supabase.co, so the first real room is the first real test. If joining fails, the player sees "Could not connect to the room".
- **Honest limits:** each player's shop runs on their own device, so scores are reported by the client and a determined cheater could edit theirs. Mitigations: scores use fixed default KPI weights, values are range-checked, a player can only post their own card. Closing a tab pauses that player's shop (browser throttling); leaving keeps their last score on the board. No reconnect after a refresh: a refreshed player continues solo.
- **Tests:** 205 passing. Overflow audit clean at 5 viewports; no visible scrollbars.

## 11. MonsoonSIM gap pass

- **Added:** credit rating AAA to DDD (late payday or overdrawn days in the last 30; worse rating means dearer loans and overdraft), seller star rating on the Web store (needs 8 orders; poor delivery cuts web conversion by up to 20%), and ROE, debt to equity, current and quick ratio on Statements.
- **Tests:** 219 passing (`tests/credit.test.js`, `tests/rating.test.js`). Morph and scrollbar checks clean.
- **Honest limits:** rating curve and star-to-conversion effect are my own model, not MonsoonSIM's hidden numbers. Parity is measured against the public spec, not hands-on use. Still missing from the original recon list: nothing on the feature table except the items marked partial.

- **Facilitator checkpoints:** the host can stop every device now, or set an automatic stop every 5 to 20 days in the room settings. Players see a waiting message; the host presses Continue (Mail, Facilitator). The Continue message carries the day, so a checkpoint a slow player has not reached yet is skipped for them. Logic in `js/live.js`, tested in `tests/live.test.js`. **Not verified:** a real two-browser room over Supabase (sandbox cannot reach it). Each device still keeps its own clock, so a stopped player's day count can differ by a few seconds from the host's.

- **Branch shops:** up to 3 extra outlets in other places (Branches tab under More). Each has a one-time fit-out (booked as equipment), daily rent and staff scaled by the place, and sells from the main shop's stock at the main shop's prices, after the main shop has sold for the day. Moving stock costs a small fee per unit. All money goes through the ledger and the reconcile check passes. Logic in `js/branch.js`, tests in `tests/branch.test.js` (224 tests in all). **Simplified on purpose:** branches have no own stock, staff roster, prices or walking customers; sales are settled once a day like the web store. Old saves load (branches default to none).

- **Client credit ratings (B2B):** each buyer has a score (AAA to DDD) that drifts daily on its own random stream, rises 2 points when it pays and falls 25 when it does not. The chance an invoice is never paid comes from the score (about 1 percent per 10 points below 100). The B2B market shows each buyer's rating and every tender card shows the expected unpaid share; you get a Credit desk mail when a buyer you are owed money by is downgraded. Tests in `tests/clientcredit.test.js` (228 in all). **Limit:** the player cannot yet demand prepayment or buy credit insurance; the rating is information and risk only. Rating bands and drift size are my own model.

- **KPI targets:** a team can set a target for any of the eight KPIs (Insights, Score) or pick Easy, Standard or Hard. The screen shows progress to target (capped at 100) and a target score. In a live room the host picks a level; it is applied to every player and locked. Targets never change the normal score or the live board, so they cannot be used to raise a rank. Tests in `tests/targets.test.js` (232 in all). **Limit:** a locked set is locked only in the client, like the other live-room scores. Preset values are my own, not MonsoonSIM's.

- **Currency and central bank:** an exchange index (1.00 neutral, range 0.80 to 1.25) multiplies the price of every supplier except the Local Co-op; a central bank rate (0.5 to 9 percent, starts at 3) pulls the index toward a level (higher rate means lower index) and scales the bank's charges on overdraft, credit and new loans. Insights, Currency shows both, a 7-day outlook with an 80 percent band, and a chart. Own random stream, so the shop's dice are untouched. Tests in `tests/currency.test.js` (238 in all), including a calibration test: over 60 seeds about 80 percent of next-week index values land inside the stated band. **Limits:** your money stays in dollars (no second currency on the books); the outlook assumes the policy rate holds; the effect sizes are my own model. One existing test (`tests/ops.test.js`, overdraft interest) now includes the policy factor.

- **Demographic marketing:** a campaign can now be aimed at everyone, seniors, adults or juniors. Each product category has a buyer mix (food mostly adults, electronics mostly juniors); each channel suits some groups better (radio seniors, social and influencers juniors, search adults). Awareness per category is the mix-weighted reach, so a well-matched campaign lifts demand about 1.5 to 2 times as hard as an unfocused one and a mismatched one much less. The Marketing screen shows awareness per age group. Unfocused campaigns and old saves behave exactly as before. Tests in `tests/demographics.test.js` (242 in all). **Limit:** the age mix, channel affinities and focus strength are my own numbers; demand itself is not split by age, only the awareness lift is.

- **Data export:** Insights, Dashboard has an Export data card with four CSV downloads: daily results, ledger journal, trial balance and B2B history. Files open in Excel (with a byte-order mark), R and Tableau. Cells that start with = + - or @ get a leading apostrophe so a spreadsheet cannot run them as formulas. Tests in `tests/export.test.js` (246 in all) parse the files back and check the journal's debits equal its credits. **Limits:** it is a file download, not a live connection to Excel or Tableau; the journal holds only the recent entries the save keeps; no import.

- **Shelf life and stock takes:** milk now has a 10 day shelf life. Age batches are rebuilt every night from the stock count (a rise is a new batch, a fall comes off the oldest first), so every way stock moves stays correct. Batches past their date are thrown away and booked to a new "Spoilage and stock loss" account at average cost; a mail warns when a loss is $20 or more. A little stock also goes missing each day (0.3 percent, own random stream) and is not booked until the player runs a stock take (Shop, Freshness): it costs $60 in staff time, books the missing units, and halves the loss rate for 14 days. Until then the stock value shown is too high by the unbooked amount, which is the point of counting. Old saves load (the new account and state are filled in). Tests in `tests/stock.test.js` (253 in all). **Limits:** shop stock only, not the warehouse, B2B stock or the web store's own catalogue; customers can still buy units that are secretly missing; only milk is perishable; loss and shelf-life numbers are my own.

- **OCEAN hiring:** People, Interview shows a shortlist of three candidates per role with five trait scores (openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness, neuroticism, 0 to 100) and a fit rating for the role (for example warehouse wants high conscientiousness, drivers want high conscientiousness and low neuroticism). Hiring from the list pays the normal fee; a good fit starts with up to 25 percent more skill, and a team with high neuroticism quits up to 40 percent more. Traits come from the person's id, so they never change. People hired with the old Hire button and all old saves behave exactly as before. The shortlist is deterministic and changes after each hire. Tests in `tests/ocean.test.js` (258 in all). **Limits:** it is a game model, not a validated personality test; the trait weights per role, skill swing and quit swing are my own numbers; traits are shown only on the Interview card and as a fit number on hired people.

- **Service jobs (RFQs and scheduling):** B2B, Services. Business clients post requests (setup, repair, training, fit-out) most days. You quote 70 to 150 percent of the market price (never above what the client will pay); the client answers the next day, with a win chance that falls as the price rises and rises with your finished jobs and customer satisfaction. A won job needs hours: each service person gives 8 hours a day (scaled by skill and morale), the support desk keeps 25 percent for tickets, and the rest goes to the queue in the order you set, at most 16 hours per job per day. You can move jobs up and down or order by deadline; the queue shows each job's projected finish day. Paid when done, less 10 percent per day late (max 50 percent); a job more than 5 days late is cancelled with a 15 percent penalty. New account "Service revenue"; service income is counted under the B2B department. A warning appears when jobs are on course to be late. Tests in `tests/services.test.js` (266 in all). **Limits:** no real calendar with named people per job (it is a shared pool of hours), no overtime option, win chance and prices are my own model, and no subcontracting.

The calendar is public: Insights, Forecast has a Calendar card listing what starts in the next 28 days, a mail arrives 3 days ahead, and the forecast removes the calendar from past sales and puts it back on future days, so a holiday is forecast, not a surprise. Only games started from now on have the calendar (a `cal` flag); old saves play exactly as before. Tests in `tests/season.test.js` (271 in all), including an exact check that the forecast is higher by the holiday multiplier. - **B2B demand outlook:** B2B, Market has a Demand outlook card. For each product buyers ask for, it shows the expected units a day (the buyer pattern, blended with the tenders seen in the last 28 days; after a week of history the history counts for most of it), the units a day you can expect to win (your real win rate once you have bid 3 times, else a stated 35 percent guess), what you already owe buyers, warehouse stock, and a stock plan for the next 14 days (`buy N` or `enough`). A calibration test runs the real tender generator for 20000 days and checks the model rate is within 10 percent for every product. Tests in `tests/b2bforecast.test.js` (275 in all). **Limits:** fixed offers are estimated in the code but left out of the table and the stock plan; no seasonality (B2B tenders are not seasonal here); the plan does not know about price, so a win rate earned at low prices may not hold at high ones.

- **Delivery quality checks (damaged goods):** Shop, Quality. Each supplier delivery can arrive with broken units. The chance is twice the supplier's miss rate (Local Co-op about 4 percent of deliveries, Pacific about 24 percent, MegaPack about 28 percent), and a hit breaks 5 to 20 percent of the units. With inspection on (25 dollars a delivery) the broken units are held as a "Supplier claims" asset, not stock. You can send them back for a credit (40 to 100 percent of the price, better suppliers pay more, the rest is booked as loss) or write them off. A claim left for 3 days lapses into a loss. With inspection off there is no fee, the loss is booked at once and cannot be claimed. Own random stream, so no other dice move. Honest limits: the numbers are this game's own model, not MonsoonSIM's; the credit is paid to cash even if the invoice is still unpaid; warehouse-to-shop transfers are never checked; only games started now have it (old saves play as before, the Quality page says so). 13 tests in `tests/quality.test.js` cover the books, claim, lapse, no-inspect, determinism and a damage-rate calibration.

- **Warehouse rent terms:** the rent dialog now has a term: daily, 7 days (8 percent off the daily rate) or 30 days (20 percent off). The term covers the whole warehouse. Picking daily while a longer term runs keeps the longer one; picking a term restarts it from today. When a term ends the rent goes back to the daily rate and a mail says so (no silent renewal). "Give up warehouse" needs an empty warehouse with nothing on the way; inside a term it books the rent still owed as rent expense, after the term it is free. Old saves have no term and pay the daily rate as before. The discounts are this game's own numbers, not MonsoonSIM's. 10 tests in `tests/whterms.test.js`.
